Essay 1. Compare the Articles of Confederation and the Constitution. Why did the Framers replace the Articles, and how did the Constitution fix its weaknesses?
What a strong answer includes
  • Articles = confederation: unicameral Congress, one vote per state, 9 of 13 to act, unanimous amendments
  • Weaknesses: no tax power, no commerce power, no executive, no national judiciary, couldn't raise an army
  • Shays' Rebellion (1786–87): no national troops; private militia — showed the need for stronger government
  • Convention (1787) called to revise but wrote a new Constitution; Great Compromise, Three-Fifths Compromise
  • Fixes: tax and commerce powers (Art. I §8), executive, judiciary (Art. III), Supremacy Clause, ratification by 9 states, Article V 2/3–3/4 amendment process
  • Why it matters: federalism and separation of powers/checks and balances balance strength with limits (Fed. 10, 51)
Essay 2. Explain how the Bill of Rights came to limit state governments. Use specific cases.
What a strong answer includes
  • 1791: Bill of Rights restricted only the federal government; Barron v. Baltimore (1833) confirmed it
  • 14th Amendment (1868) Due Process Clause; did NOT automatically apply the whole Bill of Rights
  • Selective incorporation case by case: Gitlow (1925), Near (1931), Mapp (1961), Gideon (1963), McDonald (2010)
  • 'Selective' matters: some provisions aren't incorporated (grand jury, civil jury)
  • Distinguish reverse incorporation: Bolling v. Sharpe (1954) applies equal protection to the federal government via the 5th
  • Rights aren't absolute; state constitutions may add protections
Essay 3. Trace the role of courts, Congress, and the president in advancing civil rights for Black Americans from Dred Scott to Shelby County.
What a strong answer includes
  • Dred Scott (1857): no citizenship; Congress couldn't bar slavery in territories
  • Emancipation Proclamation (1863) freed people only in rebelling areas; 13th, 14th, 15th Amendments
  • Black Codes, Jim Crow; Plessy (1896) 'separate but equal'
  • NAACP litigation → Brown (1954); Little Rock (1957): Eisenhower sends troops — courts depend on others for implementation
  • Activist strategies: boycotts, sit-ins, Freedom Rides, marches → CRA 1964 (Commerce Clause), 24th Amendment, VRA 1965, Harper (1966)
  • Shelby County (2013) struck the coverage formula, NOT the whole VRA; federalism conflicts throughout
